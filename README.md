# Paper Pilots

A party game where up to 4 players race paper airplanes through an office building. One screen (a laptop or TV) is the host: it shows the whole race for an audience. Players join from a phone or a desktop computer by scanning the QR code or opening the join link.

**Every player gets their own 3D chase view** that follows their plane. On a desktop, the controls dock in the corner (keyboard or mouse) and tuck away while you fly. On a phone, the race fills the top half of the screen and the throw pad and joystick sit underneath. Shop, results and the final standings take over the phone screen between races. Phones and desktops can play in the same room.

The host runs the only simulation. Each race it sends every player the floor and its random seed, so all screens build the exact same layout, then streams plane positions 15 times a second.

**Pick your match in the lobby:** tick which floors to play (they're always played bottom to top) and choose 1–5 races per floor. The host lobby and the solo screen both have the picker, and each device remembers its last choice. By default it's all six floors, 3 races each. Each floor is raced that many times before the elevator goes up, and **every race gets a freshly generated layout** of that floor's furniture, vents, fans, landing zones and paper clips. Floor 1 is 800 long and each floor above it is 25% longer (800 → 1,000 → 1,250 → 1,563 → 1,953 → 2,441). Planes fly the same on every floor, so the extra length is what you grow into.

**Landing zones are spread along the whole floor.** The first zone always sits 58–78 out, where a stock plane with a controlled throw can land. The rest step out toward the far wall, so a fresh plane reaches the first one or two, and reaching the far zones takes vents, clean flying and upgrades. Higher floors pay more: zone and placement payouts are 25% bigger per floor (Floor 5 pays double, the Rooftop 2.25×).

**Floor 2 and up** have moving obstacles: life-size office chairs and the mail cart in the Cubicle Farm, the snack trolley and a bouncing ball in the Break Room, a patrolling robot and a cable lift in the Server Room, and sliding glass doors and the CEO's camera drone in the Executive Suite.

**Floor 3 and up** add **hallways**: floor-to-ceiling walled corridors (1 on the Break Room, 2 on the floors above) between landing zones. Each is a single path with S-bend turns and usually a doorway you have to duck under. No dead ends, no maze: just steering.

**Career progression.** Every clip you earn counts toward a promotion: Intern → Temp → Associate → Supervisor → Manager → Director → VP → CEO. Promotions also **unlock things**:

| Title | Unlocks | Plane paper |
|---|---|---|
| Intern | Every part at Mk I | Plain copy paper |
| Temp | | Lined notebook |
| Associate | Mk II of any part | Graph paper |
| Supervisor | Mk III of any part | Legal pad |
| Manager | Mk IV of any part | Kraft paper |
| Director | Mk V of any part | Blueprint |
| VP | | Silver foil |
| CEO | | Gold foil |

Locked shop items say which title they need (for example "Mk III needs Supervisor rank"), and the phone's rank bar shows what your next promotion unlocks. Computer rivals follow the same rules.

The ladder gets steep: Temp takes 30 clips, Manager 280, Director 450, VP 700, and CEO 1,000. Early promotions come fast, but the top floors of the org chart take a near-perfect match. Your title shows on the host screen, promotions get announced after a race, and your phone shows how many clips you need for the next title. The game also tracks your personal-best distance on each floor and flags a new best.

You throw your plane from the launch desk, then glide it the rest of the way. Paper planes can't fly forever, so you want to land in the best landing zone you can reach. Zones farther away pay more paper clips. Along the way you can grab clips floating in the air, ride the floor vents to gain height, stay away from the ceiling fans that push you down, and avoid crashing into the furniture.

After every race, players spend their paper clips in the **Supply closet**. Points from race placings decide the overall winner, the **Employee of the month**.

## How to play

| | On a phone | On a keyboard |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | Drag sideways on the throw pad | Left / Right arrows |
| Angle | Slider on the left | Up / Down arrows |
| Throw | Pull back on the pad, then let go | Hold **Space**, then release |
| Steer | Joystick: left/right turns, up climbs, down dives | WASD or the arrow keys |

On the host screen, **Enter** (or Space) skips the floor intro and the results screen.

**Inverted up/down:** the Controls settings (solo screen and phone lobby) have an **Up/down** choice. *Inverted* makes the joystick and the up/down keys work like a flight sim: pull down (or press down) to climb, push up to dive. It's saved on each device.

**Don't wait too long on the desk:** 9 seconds after GO, a plane that hasn't been thrown gets a weak toss automatically. The aim screen shows an "Auto-throw in N" countdown from GO (it turns red for the last 3 seconds and the phone buzzes), and the host screen shows the last 5 seconds on that plane's name tag and racer card.

**Flying tips:** Diving builds speed. Climbing turns that speed back into height. If you climb too hard you'll stall and drop. Floor vents (the round grates with streaks rising out of them) lift you up, and ceiling fans push you down, but **both drafts speed you up**: an updraft is free height plus a little speed, and a downdraft trades height for a bigger burst of speed. Hitting the ceiling or furniture costs durability, and at zero durability your plane crumples into a ball and earns no zone payout. Zones painted on top of desks, tables, the fridge and so on only count if you land on the top surface.

**Payouts each race:** you get the landing zone's value (3 / 6 / 10 / 15 / 24 clips), plus 8 / 5 / 3 / 1 clips for placing 1st to 4th, plus any clips you picked up. Placings also give 10 / 6 / 3 / 1 points toward the overall win. A plane that crumples gets no zone or placement reward.

**Target zones.** Every landing zone is painted as a three-ring target. Where you land inside it multiplies that zone's pay:

| Ring | Pay |
|---|---|
| Bullseye (centre third) | ×2 (also scores the Bullseye trick) |
| Inner ring | ×1.5 |
| Outer ring / corners | ×1 |

Raised zones (desk tops, the fridge, a rack) are targets too.

**Ranking within a race:** the best zone wins; within the same zone, the landing closer to the centre wins; then whoever flew farther. Missing every zone ranks below any zone landing, and crumpling ranks last.

### Upgrades: your plane's loadout

Your plane has **five slots**, and each one holds one part. Every slot starts with a free stock part, and there are four parts to buy for each slot (20 in all). **Buying a part puts it straight into its slot.** Whatever it replaced goes into your **inventory**: everything you've bought stays yours for the rest of the match, so you can swap back for free between races. Only equipped parts do anything.

Each part goes up to **Mk V**. Mk II needs Associate rank, Mk III Supervisor, Mk IV Manager and Mk V Director. Each level costs ×1.3, ×3.4, ×6.5, ×8.5 and ×11 the part's base price (a Wide Glider all the way to Mk V is 1,290 clips). Mk IV and Mk V each add about half of what Mk III added, and **Mk V also switches on the part's perk** (last column). Upgrades reset every match, so everyone starts with a plain plane.

**Silver and gold.** A Mk IV part gets a silver outline and a Mk V part a gold one, with a slow glint, on the 3D plane and everywhere in the shop (part rows, Mk badge, level pips, slot cards) and on the host's upgrade chips. A Mk IV/V paper or fold outlines the whole plane, and so do the Silver Foil (VP) and Gold Foil (CEO) papers. The outline is drawn as plain colour, so it shows on every graphics setting.

**Between races the shop opens as a full-screen Hangar** on every player's screen. Your plane sits on a paper turntable, turning slowly, and you can drag it to spin it. Five slot cards under it show what's fitted and at what Mk, and you pick a slot to see its parts. On a desktop, hovering a part shows the plane with it fitted before you buy. **The shop shows exactly what you're getting.** Six stat bars (Launch, Speed, Glide, Turning, Toughness, Landing) show your current plane. Every row says what tapping it does (Buy, the next Mk level, or Equip) and how each bar would move, as green ▲ and red ▼ marks. Rows that are locked say why.

**Parts change the plane's look**, so builds are easy to tell apart on the host screen. The fold sets the silhouette: wide wings, a long narrow needle, or upturned stunt fins, and higher Mk levels make each one more extreme. Tissue paper goes see-through, glossy paper shines, and cardstock gets a visible second layer. Vellum is pale and frosted, and the Swallowtail Fold has a notched, forked tail. You can also see the pencil nose, staple, magnet, tape, flaps, bookmark ribbons, binder clip, skids, rubber band, highlighter and doodled flames.

The host's Supply closet board shows each player's equipped parts and their Mk levels.

Computer rivals each build toward a style: a glider, a fast dart, a tough brick, or a build of the newer parts (Swallowtail, Vellum, Staple Nose, Bookmark Ribbon, Highlighter). In simulated 7-floor matches (3 races each) they finish around Manager–Director with most parts at Mk I–III; only the strongest sometimes gets its main part to Mk IV, and Mk V is a goal for a player who saves for it.

### The Rooftop (Floor 6)

The top floor is the roof, under open sky. The middle half of the run isn't over a building at all: it's the gap down to the street between the start building and a second, lower building where the rest of the landing zones are.

- **The first roof** has AC units (their exhaust fans blow updrafts), water towers, satellite dishes, vent stacks and stairwell huts, with knee-high parapets along the edges. The easy first zone is here, 58–78 out as always.
- **The gap.** Rising air columns between the buildings (streaks coming up from the street) carry you across. A crosswind swings you left and right in slow pulses. Dive below the roofline for the paper clip arcs and bonus boxes down there, and gusts push you back up. Go deeper than the gusts reach and you're on your own: crash into the far building's wall, or end up on the street with no zone.
- **The far roof** is 7 lower, so the jump is a glide down. Zones 2–5 are all here, including the Stairwell Roof and AC Unit perches.
- **Pigeons.** Flocks wheel back and forth across the run, over both roofs and the gap.
- **The view.** The gap looks down on a big city park: thousands of trees, winding dirt paths with benches, and two ponds. A grid of streets with cars and towers on every block fills the skyline around it.
- At the edges of the roofs there's no wall, just a wall of wind that turns you back in without damage. The sky has no ceiling to scrape.

### Shortcuts (Floor 4 and up)

The Server Room and Executive Suite each have two risky shortcuts. Both are lined with paper clips, and both stay 150–200 long however long the floor is.

- **Window run.** One side wall has an open window (hazard tape on the sill) before one of the hallways, and a second open window after it. Fly out the first, ride the outside ledge past the hallway, and fly back in through the second. Outside, a crosswind shoves you away from the building, so you have to steer into it. Air rising up the facade holds you roughly level. Re-entering earns **Window Washer** (8 clips, part of the combo) and a speed burst. If you drift too far out, a wall of wind turns you back toward the building, with no damage. Drop too low or overshoot the re-entry window and you're **stuck outside**: crumpled, wedged on the ledge.
- **Duct run.** An open hole with a yellow trim, its grate pulled off and set beside it, is cut into the ceiling (or the floor). It leads into a tight square air duct hidden above the ceiling (or under the floor) that usually skips right over a hallway. A ceiling hole has a strong vent under it that carries you up, and the duct's suction pulls you in once you're close. A floor hole you dive into. Inside, airflow speeds you along, but the duct is barely wider than your wings and every scrape costs durability. A second open hole farther on drops (or blows) you back into the room with a speed burst and **Duct Rat** (8 clips).

Computer rivals don't take the shortcuts.

### Trick shots and streaks

Flashy flying pays **bonus paper clips**. These go to the shop and toward promotions, not race points. Each trick gets a big hand-lettered callout by the plane, and your phone shows it too.

| Trick | Clips | How |
|---|---|---|
| Close Call | 1 | Skim past furniture at speed (max 3 per flight) |
| Under the Desk | 2 | Fly under a desk or table (max 3) |
| Draft Chain | 2 | Ride a 2nd, then a 4th different vent in one flight |
| Thread the Needle | 3 | Fly through a hallway doorway, under the lintel |
| Clean Hallway | 4 | Get all the way down a hallway without touching anything |
| Bullseye | 3 | Land in the bullseye ring of a zone |
| Window Washer | 8 | Get back in through the second window on a window run |
| Duct Rat | 8 | Make it out the far end of a duct |
| Stuck It | 4 | Land on a raised zone (desk, fridge, rack top) |
| Bank Shot | 3 | Land in a zone within 3 s of bouncing off something |
| Running on Fumes | 3 | Land in a zone with a quarter of your durability or less |
| Full Circle | 4 | Bank through a complete loop in one direction (max 2) |
| Ceiling Skim | 3 | Hug the ceiling for 2 s without touching it (max 2) |
| Carpet Burner | 3 | Fly fast just above the floor for 1.5 s (max 2) |
| Zigzag | 2 | Flip your bank hard left/right 4 times within 3 s (max 2) |
| Speed Demon | 3 | Hit top speed in a dive (once per flight) |
| Stall Recovery | 3 | Stall out, then dive back to full speed without hitting anything (max 2) |
| Hang Time | 4 | Stay airborne for a long time (floor length ÷ 26 seconds) |
| Traffic Dodger | 3 | Skim past a moving obstacle (chair, cart) at speed (max 2) |
| Clip Sweep | 3 | Grab 5 paper clips within 3 s (once per flight) |
| Slipstream | 4 | Tuck in right behind a rival's plane, then pass them (max 2) |

**Combo:** tricks in the same flight chain together, with each one worth more than the last (x1, x1.5, x2, x2.5, then x3). Taking any hit breaks the chain.

**Streaks** carry across races:
- **Zone streak:** land in a zone race after race for +2, +4, +6... clips (up to +10).
- **Clean streak:** finish races without taking any damage for +2, +4... (up to +8).

Both are scaled by the floor's pay bonus and reset when you miss. The results board shows trick and streak earnings for everyone.

### Photo finish and game feel

When the top two land in the same zone within 8 ft of each other, the race ends with a **photo finish**. The screen letterboxes, a "Photo finish" stamp drops, and the last couple of seconds replay in slow motion from a low side angle, tinted like an old photo. A camera flash then reveals a pinned photo naming the winner and the margin ("by a nose" if it's under a foot).

The rest of the race has extra feedback too:
- **Speed and drafts:** the camera widens as you pick up speed. Drafts kick the view and blow air streaks around the plane, and a wind sound rises with speed.
- **Hits:** big hits freeze the action for a beat, wobble the plane, and flash red on the plane's own phone.
- **Close calls:** skimming past furniture at speed shows "Close call!" and plays a whoosh.
- **Clips and landings:** chained paper-clip pickups count up a combo with rising chimes. Zone landings throw confetti and an ink ring that grow bigger the farther the zone.
- **Running order:** live 1st–4th places slide into order on the host screen, the leader's name tag wears a paper crown, and lead changes get called out.
- **Phones:** quick color pulses and buzzes for hits, pickups and drafts.

With "reduce motion" on, the screen shake, field-of-view changes, wobble and flashes switch off.

## Solo flight (no phone needed)

On the home screen, click **Solo flight**. Pick 0–3 computer rivals, which floors to play and how many races per floor. The phone controller appears as a panel in the corner of the screen, and you can use it with the mouse or the keyboard. This is the quickest way to try out the gameplay.

**On a phone, solo is fully self-contained:** no host screen or second device needed. Open the site on the phone and tap **Solo flight**. The race fills the top half of the screen, following your plane, with the throw pad and joystick underneath. Results and the final standings take over the screen between races, and the final card has **Play again** and **Menu**. The phone goes full screen where the browser allows it, and renders at a lighter resolution to stay smooth.

## Run it locally

The game is static files with no build step. Opening `index.html` by double-clicking won't work, because browsers block ES modules and WebRTC on `file://`. Serve the folder instead:

```bash
cd paper-pilots
python3 -m http.server 8080
# then open http://localhost:8080
```

Solo mode works on `localhost` right away.

To test multiplayer on one computer, open the host in a normal window and join from a **private/incognito** window. Two tabs in the same window share a player identity, so the second tab would take over the first one's seat.

Real phones need HTTPS, so deploy first (see below) and then scan the QR code.

## Deploy to AWS Amplify (HTTPS, free tier)

**Drag and drop (easiest):**

1. Zip the `paper-pilots` folder. The zip should contain `index.html` at its top level, not inside a parent folder.
2. In the AWS Amplify console, choose **Create new app**, then **Deploy without Git**.
3. Drop the zip in. Amplify gives you an `https://….amplifyapp.com` URL.
4. To update the game later, drop in a new zip.

**From Git:** connect the repo in Amplify. The included `amplify.yml` deploys the files as-is with no build step.

Netlify, Vercel, Cloudflare Pages and GitHub Pages all work the same way with no configuration.

Join links look like `https://your-site/?room=ABCD`. The QR code is built from whatever address the host page is on, so it always points at the right place.

## Before real use: the matchmaking server

Phones connect directly to the host (peer-to-peer WebRTC). A small "broker" server introduces them to each other first. Out of the box the game uses the free public PeerJS broker. That's fine for playing with friends, but it has no uptime guarantee.

For anything public, run your own PeerServer (`npm i -g peer` and then `peerjs --port 9000`, or use the official Docker image) behind HTTPS. Then put its address in `js/shared/config.js`:

```js
export const PEER_OPTIONS = { host: 'peer.yourdomain.com', port: 443, secure: true, path: '/' };
```

**Networks:** players on the same Wi-Fi connect directly. Players on very different networks (strict corporate or mobile carrier NATs) may need a TURN server. You can add one with `config: { iceServers: [...] }` in `PEER_OPTIONS`.

## Good to know

- **The host screen is not a player.** A 4-player game means 4 player devices (phones or desktops) plus the host screen. You can start a race with just 1 player.
- **Reconnecting:** if a phone locks or drops, reopen the same link (or let the page reconnect on its own). The player gets the same seat, name, clips and upgrades back. On the host, a dropped player's card is greyed out while they're away, and their plane keeps gliding on its own.
- **Closing the host tab ends the room.** The browser will ask you to confirm if a game is in progress.
- **Reduced motion:** if the operating system's "reduce motion" setting is on, the game turns off screen shake, line wobble and most animations. Everything is still playable.

## Project layout

```
index.html            all screens, CDN tags, Three.js import map
css/style.css         cork board + paper art direction, motion system
css/player-view.css   layout for the players' own 3D view (desktop dock, phone split screen)
js/main.js            picks host / solo / phone from the URL
js/net.js             PeerJS host-authoritative networking (rooms, reconnect, liveness)
js/ui.js              screen switching, roster sticky notes, toast
js/shared/            config, colours, upgrade catalogue, solo local link
js/game/              pure rules: flight sim, floors, bots, payouts (no DOM)
js/host/              shared screen: flow, 3D renderer, ink shader, HUD, sound
js/client/            players: join flow, controller, and the player's own 3D view (view.js)
```

To tune the game, edit:

- `js/game/sim.js` for flight physics (`SIM` constants)
- `js/game/floors.js` for floor themes and the random layout generator (zone distance bands, the guaranteed-clear lane, vent and fan counts)
- `js/shared/upgrades.js` for upgrade costs, per-level effects and `MAX_LEVEL`
- `js/game/rules.js` for payouts and `RACES_PER_FLOOR` (the default races per floor)
- `js/shared/setup.js` for the lobby's floor and races-per-floor picker

The rendering style is a custom post-process in `js/host/ink.js`. It draws ink outlines from depth and normal edges, adds pencil crosshatching in the shadows, and puts paper grain over everything, with lines that "boil" at 6 fps like a hand-drawn animation.
